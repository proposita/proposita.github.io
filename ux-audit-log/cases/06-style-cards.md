# Case 06 — Style cards: view formats, color/size/price consistency, and image browsing

**Status:** Closed — minor issues only. Across desktop, tablet (768px), and mobile (375px), the card view formats work correctly and are genuinely distinct at every width, color/size/availability/price data agrees exactly across formats and widths, and a style's photos across all of its available colors can be browsed and enlarged from the card itself everywhere tested. The real gaps found are all small: desktop's color swatches don't stay in sync with whichever color's photo is currently being browsed and can't jump straight to a color; tablet requires one extra click (opening the image gallery) to browse colors instead of desktop's direct hover-arrows; mobile's "..." menu lists "Show Favorites" twice (cosmetic, both stay in sync); and two of the card/header's icons (the drawer-entry icon, and mobile's "..." icon) turned out to be static images outside the app's real icon system, with a measurable color mismatch and — across the wider product — no single consistent "more options" icon at all. A first tablet pass wrongly reported much larger issues — the three views rendering identically, and hover controls being unreachable — which didn't survive a careful retest; see Notes for what happened and why.

**Priority:** Low — everything core to this case works as expected across all three widths. Nothing here blocks browsing, comparing colors, switching views, favoriting, or reading a style's real data at any width.

## Scope

- **Area:** Card-level testing of style cards on a linesheet report: the three advertised View formats (Gallery, Standard, Extended), whether color/size/availability/price data agrees across formats, whether a style's photos can be browsed and enlarged for each of its available colors, and how a card hands off into the right-side detail drawer. Per the UX Assessment Lead, the drawer's own content and behavior are explicitly out of scope here — that's Case 07.
- **Screen:** `/reports/3771` (the Fall 2026 linesheet report), the same style-card grid tested in Case 02, this time focused on the cards themselves rather than the report's search/header controls.
- **Interaction boundary:** Desktop tested using the user's own real, already-authenticated Chrome session; the tablet and mobile follow-ups both used Claude's built-in browser with true viewport emulation (768×1024, then 375×812), also on an already-authenticated session. Purely read-only aside from a few deliberate, reverted checks: a style was favorited (on desktop, again during the tablet pass, and again during the mobile pass) to confirm the star control and the "Show Favorites" filter work together, then un-favorited immediately after each time, restoring the report to its original state. A "SELECT ALL" (favorite everything) confirmation dialog was also triggered once by mistake during the tablet pass and cancelled without confirming. No quantities were entered, no cart action taken, no order created. The detail drawer was opened only to confirm its two entry points work correctly with the right product and color pre-selected — not otherwise exercised, since evaluating the drawer itself is Case 07's job.
- **Session:** Sixth case of this audit round, covering desktop, tablet (768px), and mobile (375px). An initial desktop pass misread two of this case's behaviors, and an initial tablet pass separately misread three more; all were corrected after the UX Assessment Lead flagged them, and the corrected, verified behavior is what's documented below (see Notes for what changed and why). The mobile follow-up used the assessment methodology's standard collaborative approach for this browser's sub-768px click limitation.

## Steps — the three View formats

- Confirmed via the page's own accessibility labels that the three icons next to "View" are, in order: "Gallery view", "Standard card view", and "Extended list view". On a fresh page load, "Gallery view" is the active, default format: a photo-forward card per style, with a color-swatch row, name, SKU, and price beneath it, three cards per row at this width.
- Switched to "Extended list view": one row per style, full-width, one card per line — the color name appears as its own text label per row (e.g. "Black", "Dark Navy"), price is repeated on every row, and per-size (XS–XL) quantities plus an EXPCT. (expected date) column are shown for every color.
- Switched to "Standard card view": three cards per row, each already showing its own inline per-color size/availability breakdown (matching Extended's numbers) beneath the photo — but more compact: no color-name text label per row (just the swatch color chip), and the price shown once at the top of the card rather than repeated per row.
- Switched back to "Gallery view" from Standard: correctly returned to the collapsed, photo-forward card layout — confirming Gallery does work as a real, distinct format; it simply produces no visible change when clicked while it's already the active view (which is the case on a fresh page load), which is what caused it to initially read as broken. Re-verified the active-state highlight moves correctly between all three icons as each is clicked.

- Opened the lightbox by clicking a card's photo directly: same content as the hover-arrow browsing, in a larger overlay, with its own left/right arrows and page counter (e.g. "2 / 14" for Short Sleeve Crew) — paging through it the same way confirmed it also crosses from one color into the next, matching the card-level hover behavior.
- Clicked the small icon to the right of the price at the bottom of the "Short Sleeve Crew" card: opened the right-side detail drawer, correctly showing "Short Sleeve Crew / PA1142" with "Dark Navy" pre-selected — matching the color the card was displaying at the time. This is the "click the card or its icon" entry point the UX Assessment Lead asked to have confirmed; the drawer's own contents were not evaluated further here (Case 07).
- Hovering a card and clicking its favorite star toggled it on (confirmed via a close-up check that the star filled solid); toggling the page's "Show Favorites" control at top right correctly filtered the grid down to only that one favorited style, and its own label changed to "Show All" while active — a nice contextual touch, since the label always describes what clicking it next will do. Reverted both (un-favorited the style, switched the filter back) to leave the report as found.
- Compared the Standard view's inline per-color table against Extended view's full table for two styles (3/4 Sleeve Boatneck, Short Sleeve Crew): every color, every per-size quantity, every EXPCT. date, and every price matched exactly between the two formats, including both views correctly showing two separate rows for a color with two incoming inventory batches (e.g. Black arriving both "In Stock" and again on a later "Aug 01" date).

## Findings

### Finding 1 — The color swatches don't indicate which color is currently being shown via the arrow-browsed photos

Paging through a card's photos with the hover arrows (or the lightbox) does cross from one available color into the next, but nothing in the swatch row reflects that: the swatches look the same — no highlight, border, or other selected-state change — no matter which color the visible photo has moved to. A user relying on the swatch row as a quick reference for "what color am I looking at" has to instead read that from the photo itself.

**Heuristic relevance:** Visibility of system status (the interface has this information — it knows which color is showing — but doesn't surface it in the one place a user would naturally look).

**Suggested direction:** Highlight or otherwise mark whichever swatch corresponds to the color currently shown by the arrows/lightbox, and update it live as the user pages through.

**Priority note:** Low — this doesn't block anything, since the photo itself always shows the true current color; it's a missed opportunity for a clearer status cue, not an error.

### Finding 2 — Clicking a color swatch doesn't jump to that color's photo directly

The swatches look like they should let you pick a color and see it immediately, but clicking one only opens or closes the inline size/availability table — it has no effect on which photo is displayed. To actually see a specific color's photo, the only path found was paging through the hover arrows (or the lightbox) sequentially from wherever the card currently is, which could mean several clicks for a color that happens to be photographed later in the sequence.

**Heuristic relevance:** Match between system and the real world (a row of clickable-looking color swatches implies clicking one shows that color); flexibility and efficiency of use.

**Suggested direction:** Consider wiring a swatch click to jump the photo (and, ideally, the arrow position) directly to that color's first photo, rather than requiring sequential paging to reach it.

**Priority note:** Low — full color browsing is confirmed possible via the arrows, so nothing is actually inaccessible; this is an efficiency gap, not a blocker.

Switching between all three, in every direction, correctly updated both the layout and the active-state highlight on the View icons every time.
- A style's photos across every one of its available colors can be browsed directly from the card — no need to open the drawer just to see what a style looks like in a different color. Confirmed crossing from Black into Dark Navy (and further) on one style by simply paging the hover arrows, and the same full sequence is also reachable via the lightbox.
- The lightbox and the card's own hover-arrow browsing show the same content and stay in agreement — paging counts and image order matched between the two wherever compared.
- The lightbox handles both ends of the range gracefully: a style with many photos (14 confirmed) pages cleanly with a running counter, and a style with only one photo doesn't error or dead-end, it simply has nothing further to page to.
- The favorite star and the "Show Favorites" filter work correctly together end to end: starring a card immediately reflects as a filled star, the filter correctly narrows the grid to just the favorited item(s), and the filter's own label swaps to describe its next action ("Show Favorites" ↔ "Show All") rather than staying static — a small but genuinely helpful touch.

Both also correctly handle a color with two separate incoming inventory batches, showing it as two distinct rows with their own quantities and dates rather than merging or dropping either.
- The small icon next to a card's price reliably opens the right-side detail drawer, correctly scoped to the exact product and the color the card was displaying at the time — a clean, working entry point into drawer detail.

## Findings

### Finding 3 — At 768px, hovering a card's photo shows the fullscreen icon and favorite star but not the color-browsing arrows; browsing colors from the grid means opening the image gallery first

On desktop, hovering a card's photo reveals a fullscreen icon, a favorite star, and left/right arrows that page directly through every available color's photos. At 768px, the same hover still reveals the fullscreen icon and the star, but not the arrows — the only way to page from one color's photo to the next is to first open the full-screen image gallery (via the fullscreen icon, or by clicking the photo directly), which does have working arrows and correctly crosses between colors. So the capability itself is fully intact at this width; what's missing is the quick, no-extra-click preview that hovering gives on desktop.

**Heuristic relevance:** Consistency across breakpoints; efficiency of use (an extra step — opening the gallery — is required at 768px for something desktop does in place).

**Suggested direction:** Low-effort option: no change needed, since the gallery is one click away and clearly discoverable (the fullscreen icon is visible on hover). If more parity with desktop is wanted, consider showing simplified arrows directly on the card at this width too, sized for a narrower layout.

**Priority note:** Low — nothing is hidden or broken; a user can still reach every color's photo in one extra click via the gallery, and that gallery is easy to find.

## Findings

### Finding 4 — The mobile "..." menu lists "Show Favorites" twice

Opening the "..." (more options) menu at 375px shows two separate "Show Favorites" toggle rows, one directly under the other, both doing exactly the same thing. Checked in the page's own markup: both rows are genuinely separate elements (not a visual illusion), sharing the same HTML id — which is itself invalid markup, two elements should never share one id — and both are wired to the same underlying state, so they never go out of sync with each other; toggling either one toggles both, and the grid filters correctly either way. So nothing is functionally broken, but a user opening this menu sees a redundant, slightly confusing duplicate control.

**Heuristic relevance:** Consistency and standards (per Case 02's own request from the UX Assessment Lead, filed under Area 3 — Responsive and accessibility spot checks); error prevention (a duplicated toggle invites doubt about whether the two controls really are the same, even though they are).

**Suggested direction:** Remove the duplicate row from the mobile dropdown's markup, keeping a single "Show Favorites" entry there.

**Priority note:** Low — both toggles stay in sync and the feature works correctly either way; this is a visual/markup cleanup, not a functional defect.

## Steps — icon design consistency (cross-width)

- Before closing this case, the UX Assessment Lead flagged two specific icons by eye — the card's drawer-entry icon (the "calculator with a rectangle behind it," seen at the bottom-right of every card next to the price) and the mobile report header's "..." (more options) icon — as visually out of step with the rest of the interface's iconography: a different color and a heavier line weight on the first, and a glyph that "feels out of system" on the second. Rather than answering from a visual impression alone, inspected both directly in the page's own code to confirm or correct that read with evidence.
- **Drawer icon:** found it's rendered via `<img src="assets/images/icons/style_drawer.svg">` — a static image reference, not an inline vector participating in the app's live styling. Fetched that SVG file directly: its shapes are solid filled geometry (no `stroke` at all) with the fill color hard-coded inside the file itself, `fill="#90807B"`. Compared this against the card's other icons in the same corner — the favorite star and the fullscreen/expand icon — both of which are inline SVGs using `stroke="currentColor"` at a thin `1.5` stroke-width, which inherit the app's live icon color, computed as `rgb(92, 79, 72)` (`#5C4F48`) wherever checked.
- **Mobile "..." icon:** found it's `<img src="assets/images/icons/small_actions.png" alt="Actions">` inside a circular button — a PNG raster file, not a vector at all. For comparison, checked how the rest of the app's real icon system works: it's built around a reusable `<svg-icon src="....svg">` Angular component that renders inline, resolution-independent vector icons (used for the panel-close ✕, the info icon, and others checked in this same session) — a structurally different, more capable mechanism than a plain `<img>`.
- Checked whether an equivalent "more options" icon exists anywhere else in the product, to test the UX Assessment Lead's sense that this glyph is unique to this one spot. Navigated to ADMINISTRATION → Manage Reports (already reviewed for its own "Actions" column in Case 05) and inspected its row-level kebab icon: it loads yet another distinct file, `assets/images/icons/context_menu_dots_32x32.png` — vertical dots with no circular background, visually different from the mobile "..." icon's horizontal dots in a circle. Case 04 previously documented that Manage Users' own "Actions" column uses a third, completely different glyph (a single edit pencil) for the same column label.

## Findings

### Finding 5 — Two icons on this screen (and a third documented elsewhere) don't participate in the app's real icon system, with measurable visual consequences

Two specific icons the UX Assessment Lead flagged by eye both check out as genuinely inconsistent with the rest of the interface, for a concrete, shared technical reason: both are static image files (one SVG loaded via `<img>`, one PNG) instead of the inline, `currentColor`-aware vector icons the rest of the app's icon system uses.

The card's drawer-entry icon (`style_drawer.svg`, loaded via `<img>`) has its fill color hard-coded inside the file itself (`#90807B`), which does not match the app's actual live icon color (`#5C4F48`, computed from the neighboring favorite-star and expand icons) — a real, measurable color mismatch, not just a subjective impression. It's also drawn as solid filled shapes rather than the thin (`1.5`px) stroke-outline style the neighboring icons use, which is what reads as a "heavier line" even though technically there's no stroke at all, just solid fill. Because it's a flat image rather than a live vector, it also can never pick up a hover state, an active-state color, or a future theme change the way the rest of the icon system automatically does.

The mobile report header's "..." icon (`small_actions.png`) is a PNG raster inside a circular button, structurally outside the app's `<svg-icon>`-based icon system (used elsewhere for the same screen's close and info icons). Checking whether this same "more options" concept appears elsewhere in the product turned up not one consistent icon but three unrelated ones: this circled-horizontal-dots PNG on mobile, a different vertical-dots PNG with no circle on Manage Reports' row-level Actions column (`context_menu_dots_32x32.png`), and a plain edit-pencil icon on Manage Users' own Actions column (documented separately in Case 04). So this isn't one icon that drifted from a shared standard — there's no single shared "more options" icon in the product to drift from in the first place.

**Heuristic relevance:** Consistency and standards (both the UX Assessment Lead's specific catches, and the broader pattern of three different "more options" glyphs found across screens); recognition over recall (a solid, differently-colored glyph and a uniquely-styled "..." button both ask the user to learn one-off visual meanings rather than recognizing a pattern already established elsewhere in the product).

**Suggested direction:** Bring the drawer-entry icon into the same inline-SVG icon system as the rest of the card (matching its stroke weight and letting it inherit the live icon color via `currentColor`) rather than loading it as a static image. Separately, standardize on one "more options" icon and reuse it everywhere the product needs that concept — mobile's report header, Manage Reports' row actions, and (paired with Case 04/05's existing findings) Manage Users' row actions — instead of the three unrelated glyphs currently in use.

**Priority note:** Low — nothing here is broken or blocks a task; this is a visual-consistency and design-system finding, worth fixing as part of a broader icon cleanup rather than urgently.
